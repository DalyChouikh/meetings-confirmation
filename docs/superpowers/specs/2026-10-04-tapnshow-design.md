# TapNShow — Design Spec

- **Date:** 2026-10-04
- **Author:** DalyChouikh (with Claude)
- **Status:** Draft — awaiting review
- **Product name:** TapNShow (`tapnshow.vercel.app`; name lives in a single `APP_NAME` config constant)
- **Origin:** GDG on Campus ISSAT Sousse; built as a general-purpose, public, free, brand-neutral tool

---

## 1. Problem & Goals

### Problem
Groups (university clubs first, but any team or person) agree on meeting times in busy chat threads (e.g. Messenger). Members who don't check the thread miss the confirmed time and arrive late or not at all. Absence/lateness justifications are collected through a separate Google Form and land in a sheet the management committee reviews later.

### Goals
1. A member who never opens the chat thread still receives the meeting by email and can put it in their calendar in one tap.
2. Members answer **Attending / Late / Absent** (or simpler modes) from a personal link, with reasons where the group requires them.
3. Organizers and their committee see who is coming, who is late and why, without chasing anyone — in an in-app dashboard and optionally a synced Google Sheet.
4. Usable by any group or individual, not only GDG ISSAT Sousse.
5. Everything is free to run.

### Success criteria
- An organizer can go from "time confirmed in chat" to "invites sent" in under 2 minutes on a phone.
- A member can respond in one tap (Attending) or under 30 seconds (Late/Absent with reason).
- Responses appear in the dashboard immediately and in the Google Sheet within a few minutes.
- No member is ever emailed twice for the same invite/update.

### Non-goals (v1)
- Recurring meetings (a "Duplicate meeting" action covers the need).
- File attachments (links in the agenda instead).
- A general form builder.
- Voting on the meeting time (stays in the group's chat).
- Paid plans, ads, or any paid infrastructure.

## 2. Hard Constraints

| Constraint | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **Free only** — nothing paid, ever (incl. domain renewals) | Vercel Hobby, Supabase Free, Gmail-based sending, no custom domain |
| **Vercel Hobby is non-commercial only** | No payments or ads; donations allowed |
| **Vercel Hobby cron: once/day, ±59 min** | Minute-level scheduling must come from Supabase Cron (spike S1) or an alternative |

| **No Server Actions; all reads & writes via API route handlers** | Client shells + TanStack Query; Zod schemas shared; explicit CSRF checks |
| **No emojis** in UI, mockups, emails, or notifications | Phosphor icons in "sticker" tiles |
| **Repo rules** (`.agents/01-core-rules.md`) | bun, strict types (no `any`/`unknown`), date-fns, JSDoc on public APIs, unit tests for all new code, Playwright verification of UI, SQL only in dedicated query files, no hardcoded values, logger instead of `console.log` |

## 3. Users & Roles

| Actor | Account | Capabilities |
|---|---|---|
| **Owner** (one per workspace) | Required | Everything an Admin can do, plus: delete the workspace, transfer ownership (to an existing Admin), invite/promote/demote/remove Admins. Exactly one Owner always exists; the Owner must transfer before leaving |
| **Admin** (e.g. club lead) | Required | Everything in a workspace: meetings, lists, settings, integrations, inviting/removing Viewers and their `can_check_in` |
| **Viewer** (e.g. Team Management committee) | Required | Read-only: meetings, responses, per-member history, exports, Sheet link |
| **Member (no account)** | None | Responds through a personal link from the email |
| **Member (with account)** | Optional | Sees upcoming invites across all workspaces, changes answers, enables push notifications, exports/deletes their data |
| **Platform** | — | Sends system emails only (sign-in codes, Viewer invites) through Supabase custom SMTP: a dedicated platform Gmail now, Resend once a domain is owned. Never sends meeting invites |

A user can be Admin/Viewer in several workspaces and a member of others with the same account.

Per-member history in the person sheet (Lists, or a name on a meeting page) with period chips (default last 3 months); an **Attendance** view in Lists (People / Attendance switch) summarizes everyone, filtered by list chips. Export CSV and Excel (decided 2026-10-08) |

The roster, the Audience step and the Attendance table keep loading everything at once (hard cap 2,000 contacts, virtualized) so search, list filters and select-all stay on the device; #174 measures them at 2,000 and switches only if slow (owner decision 2026-10-08) |
| Meeting fields | Title, date/time, duration, timezone, location text and/or meeting URL (in-person/online/hybrid), Markdown agenda, optional response deadline |
| Edits after sending (M6) | **Edit** reopens the wizard's Details and Answers steps in edit mode, then **Review changes** (old → new, who is emailed, quota line) and a confirm dialog; nothing is saved before that. The answer type and the Late delay chips are fixed once sent (cancel and duplicate to change them). Date or time change → update email to everyone invited and every answer flagged **to reconfirm** (answers kept); duration change → update email to everyone invited, answers kept; place change → update email to everyone except people who said they can't come; title, agenda, deadline or note only → no email unless "Email everyone about this change" is switched on. People with a calendar copy get the calendar update **inside** the update email (one email, not two). Editing closes at the start (decided 2026-10-09) |
| Cancel and delete (M6) | **Cancel** (before the start, confirm dialog) emails everyone invited (calendar removal inside for calendar holders), drops unsent invites, updates and reminders, and keeps the meeting with a **Cancelled** label: answers stay viewable and exportable but do not count in History or Attendance; no un-cancel (Duplicate instead). **Delete** exists for cancelled meetings once their cancellation emails are out and removes the meeting, its answers and their history (decided 2026-10-09) |
| Reminders (M6) | Per meeting, pre-filled from Meeting defaults: **people who haven't answered** (or are to reconfirm) X h before the deadline, or before the start without one (Off, 1, 2, 6, **24**, 48 h); **Going and Late** X h before the start (Off, 1, **2**, 6, 24 h). Announcements have none. One timer per reminder per meeting picks the eligible people when it fires; each email re-checks just before sending. **Nudge**: Owners and Admins email everyone who hasn't answered, at most once every 12 h per meeting (decided 2026-10-09). Web Push for account holders arrives with M8 |
| Check-in (M6) | From the start, Owners, Admins and Viewers with `can_check_in` mark each invited person Present / Late / Absent on the meeting page (meetings that ask for answers, not cancelled; editable any time after). A tap saves; the declared answer shows as a dashed hint; "Mark the rest as they said" fills the unmarked (No reply → Absent). History and Attendance count the check-in where one exists, else the answer (Present counts as Going); rows show both ("Said going · Was absent"); exports add Checked in and Checked in by (decided 2026-10-09). A check-in always shows next to the answer, also when they agree ("Going · Was present"); Late at the door can carry minutes from the meeting's delay choices or Other (1–240), pre-picked from the declared delay, and exports add Was late by (min) (owner, 2026-10-10, #257) |
| Responses storage | Supabase is the source of truth; in-app dashboard + CSV/Excel export always; Google Sheets sync optional per workspace (M7) |
| Google Sheet (M7) | One-way live copy (app → sheet), turned on by the **Owner** with **any Google account they pick** (the sheet lives in that account's Drive; changing the sending Gmail never breaks it). One spreadsheet per workspace, **one tab per calendar year** of meetings ("2026"), newest first, each in the Excel export look; a year over 10,000 rows splits at meeting boundaries ("2026 · 3 Jan – 28 Apr"). One row per invited person per meeting with the Attendance Details columns; meetings told apart by a Meeting cell that carries the date, alternating bands per meeting and a **Meeting filter box** (Sheets slicer; each viewer's choice is private to them). Every sent meeting that asks for answers, upcoming and past; cancelled ones stay, marked and greyed; announcements and drafts never. The tab is **redrawn** from the database about a minute after a change (manual edits and deleted rows come back). If the file is trashed or deleted or access is lost, the sheet **pauses** and the Owner is told in plain words; nothing is recreated behind their back (decided 2026-10-10, §7.17) |
| Sending | Meeting invites, updates and reminders are sent **only from the workspace's sender Gmail** (`gmail.send`). **No shared fallback sender**: drafting works without Gmail, but sending requires connecting it (decided 2026-10-05). **One sender Gmail per workspace; only the Owner connects or replaces it**; every Admin's sends go out from it; it stays after an ownership transfer until replaced, and the person who connected it can always disconnect it (decided 2026-10-07). From line: `"<Workspace name>" <sender Gmail>`. Each meeting's emails are sent into **one Gmail thread** so the sender's Sent folder shows one conversation per meeting instead of one row per recipient (tested 2026-10-07, §9); the connect screen suggests connecting the group's own Gmail rather than a personal one. A platform sender (Resend/Brevo free tiers) was rejected for now: it needs an owned domain and its 100–300/day cap would be shared by the whole platform. Viewer invites: emailed by the platform sender **or** copied as a single-use link; either way the invite names an email and only that verified email can accept it (7-day expiry; Resend/Copy issues a new token). |
| Meeting wizard (M4) | Four steps saved as a server draft on every step change: **Details** (date and time from popover pickers, duration chips, In person / Online / Both, Markdown agenda) → **Audience** (lists first, then untick individuals; "Add people" adds several non-roster people at once, saved to the roster by default) → **Responses** (mode and its settings, optional deadline, reminders from M6) → **Review** (summary, email preview, sender and quota lines) → **confirm dialog** ("Send 30 invites from club@gmail.com now?") → meeting page with live send progress. "Invite more people" after sending emails only the new people. Response settings are pre-filled from **Settings > Meeting defaults** |
| Confirmations | Important actions (send, cancel, disconnect, ownership changes, deletes without Undo) open a confirm dialog that names the consequence (decided 2026-10-07) |
| Language | English at launch; all strings and email templates through next-intl from day one |
| Privacy | Admins + Viewers of the workspace see reasons; members never see each other; account deletion rule in §11 |
| Visual style | **Soft Neobrutalism** — thick outlines, rounded corners, "pressable" drop shadows, pastels; light + dark following system |
| Motion | **Expressive** — springy, staggered entrances, tilt-on-hover, drop-in chips, CONFIRMED stamp + shake + confetti; reduced-motion users get a snappy fallback |

## 5. Architecture

```
            ┌──────────────── Vercel (Hobby) ─────────────────┐
 Browser ──►│ Next.js App Router                              │
 (PWA)      │  ├─ client pages (TanStack Query)               │
            │  ├─ /api/**  route handlers (all reads/writes)  │
            │  │    └─ query modules (SQL lives here only)     │
            │  └─ /api/internal/dispatch  (secret-protected)  │
            └───────────────┬─────────────────▲───────────────┘
                            │                 │ HTTP every minute
                            ▼                 │ (Supabase Cron + pg_net)
            ┌──────────── Supabase (Free) ────┴───────────────┐
            │ Postgres + RLS · Auth · Cron                    │
            │ outbox_jobs ← enqueued by API routes            │
            └─────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
 Dispatcher ──► EmailSender (GmailApiSender)
 Sheet worker ──► Google Sheets + Drive APIs (drive.file)
            ──► Web Push (VAPID, M8)
```

- **Single TypeScript codebase**, run with bun locally, deployed to Vercel on Node.js.
- **All data access via API routes.** Pages are client components; TanStack Query handles caching, optimistic updates, and refetch after mutations.
- **Outbox pattern** for every email (invites, calendar, updates, cancellations, reminders). The Google Sheet is not a job: a per-workspace "changed years" marker is drained by its own worker (§8, M7).
- **Scheduler:** Supabase Cron + `pg_net` call the dispatcher every minute (S1 passed), on **production only**: Gmail connect needs a registered redirect URI, so, like Google sign-in, sending works on localhost and production; preview deployments can draft but not send. The cron job only makes the HTTP call when jobs are due.
- **Data access:** supabase-js with generated database types, used only inside `src/server/queries/*`; multi-step/transactional operations (send meeting, claim jobs, rate-limit check) are Postgres functions called via RPC. No ORM, so RLS applies through the user's session.
- **Environments (two free Supabase projects):**
  | Env | Database | Used by |
  |---|---|---|
  | Local | Local Supabase via Docker (`supabase start`) | Development, integration and RLS tests |
  | Preview | Supabase project #2 | Vercel preview deployments |
  | Production | Supabase project #1 | `tapnshow.vercel.app` |

  Migrations live in `supabase/migrations/` and are applied to Preview then Production. Docker Desktop's WSL integration must be enabled before the first task that needs a local database.
- **Monitoring:** Sentry free tier (5k errors/month, 30-day retention, 1 user) for API routes and the dispatcher; structured logs via the logger utility; `outbox_jobs.last_error` for per-job failures.

## 6. Data Model

All tables have RLS enabled. Timestamps are `timestamptz`. Emails are stored normalized (trimmed, lower-cased).

### Organizers & workspaces
- **`profiles`** — `user_id` (PK, → `auth.users` on delete cascade), `display_name` (nullable; asked once after sign-in when empty), `avatar_url`, `locale`, `last_workspace_id` (nullable → `workspaces`, on delete set null), timestamps. Created by a trigger on `auth.users` whose function lives in a private (non-exposed) schema; name/picture copied from the Google identity for display only (never used for authorization).
- **`workspaces`** — `id`, `name`, `slug` (unique; name slug + random suffix, never changes), `timezone`, `locale`, timestamps (M2); M4 adds `sender_connection_id` (nullable → `google_connections`, on delete set null; written only by the Owner) and the meeting defaults edited in Settings > Meeting defaults: `default_response_mode`, `default_delay_options` (minutes), `default_reason_required`, `default_comments_enabled`, `default_footer_note`, `default_duration_minutes`, `default_online_text`, `default_meeting_url` (the usual online place, copied into new meetings; 2026-10-08); M6 adds `default_reminder_pending_hours` (24) and `default_reminder_going_hours` (2), null = off.
- **`workspace_roles`** — (`workspace_id`, `user_id`) PK, `role` (`owner` | `admin` | `viewer`), `can_check_in` (boolean; Viewers only — the single write a Viewer may perform). Exactly one `owner` per workspace (unique partial index + functions that never leave zero).
- **`platform_admins`** — `user_id` — platform operators (initially only the project owner). Created in M9 with the admin page.
- **`workspace_invites`** — `id`, `workspace_id`, `email` (normalized), `role` (`admin` | `viewer`), `token_hash` (SHA-256 of a 256-bit token, shown once), `invited_by`, `expires_at` (+7 days), `accepted_at`, `accepted_by`, `revoked_at`, timestamps. At most one open invite per (`workspace_id`, `email`).
- **Access pattern (M2):** reads through RLS (`is_member(workspace, roles[])` helper in a private schema). Single-row edits (rename, timezone) through RLS UPDATE policies with `WITH CHECK`. Membership and ownership changes only through Postgres functions — `create_workspace`, `transfer_ownership`, `change_role`, `remove_member`, `leave_workspace`, `create_invite`, `revoke_invite`, `accept_invite`, `delete_workspace` — no direct table writes (see §11 for the `SECURITY DEFINER` exception).

### People & lists
- **`contacts`** — `id`, `workspace_id`, `email` (unique per workspace), `full_name` (required, 1–120 chars, trimmed), `user_id` (nullable link once the person has an account with this verified email; filled in M8), `unsubscribed_at` + `unsubscribed_via` (`link` | `report`; set from M4 by the person only — Admins cannot clear it and a re-import does not), `is_adhoc` (true for people added in the wizard with "Save to roster" off: hidden from the roster and lists, kept for history, flipped to false if later added to the roster; set from M4), timestamps.
- **`lists`** — `id`, `workspace_id`, `name` (1–60 chars; unique per workspace **case-insensitively**), timestamps.
- **`list_contacts`** — (`list_id`, `contact_id`) PK, both `on delete cascade`: deleting a list never deletes people; deleting a contact (a hard delete, §11) removes its memberships.
- **Access pattern (M3):** RLS + explicit `GRANT`s on all three tables: SELECT for Owner/Admin/Viewer, INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE for Owner/Admin (`private.is_member`). Single edits (rename, change email, create/rename/delete a list, delete a contact) are plain RLS writes. Multi-row operations are `SECURITY INVOKER` functions (RLS still applies): `import_contacts(workspace, rows jsonb, dry_run)` and `set_contact_lists(contact, list_ids)`. Caps are enforced by statement-level `AFTER INSERT` triggers that take a per-workspace advisory lock and compare against `app_limits` (`contacts_per_workspace_max` 2000, `lists_per_workspace_max` 50), so every write path is covered.
- **`import_contacts`:** normalizes and validates each row; merges rows with the same email inside the input (lists united, last non-empty name wins); classifies each row as `new` / `updated` (different non-empty name or added lists) / `unchanged` / `invalid` (with a reason: missing or malformed email, name over 120 chars, list name over 60 chars, or no name for an email not yet in the roster — an empty name for an existing contact keeps the stored one); a merged row also reports the input row numbers it absorbed, which the preview counts as "merged duplicates"; resolves list names case-insensitively and creates unknown ones. `dry_run = true` returns per-row outcomes, totals and new list names and writes no contacts or lists (it only counts against `import_previews_per_user_per_hour`, 120, so previews cannot be used to load the database); `dry_run = false` applies the same logic in one transaction and counts against `imports_per_user_per_hour` (30), except a one-row commit ("+ Add"), which counts against `contact_adds_per_user_per_hour` (300) so adding people by hand is not capped by the import budget (decided 2026-10-07). A commit that fails (e.g. `contacts_limit_reached`) rolls back its own rate-limit hit, so failed commits are not budgeted; accepted (#119): every UI commit follows a budgeted dry run, and request-level limits and the statement timeout bound direct abuse. At most `import_rows_max` (2000) rows per call. "+ Add" in the roster calls it with one row, so adding an existing email merges.

### Meetings
- **`meetings`** — `id`, `workspace_id`, `title`, `agenda_md`, `starts_at`, `duration_minutes`, `timezone`, `location_mode` (`in_person` | `online` | `hybrid`), `location_text`, `online_text` (where online in words, ≤ 200; 2026-10-08), `meeting_url`, `response_mode` (`announcement` | `rsvp` | `attendance`), `response_deadline`, `delay_options` (minutes), `reason_required`, `comments_enabled`, `footer_note`, `status` (`draft` | `scheduled` | `cancelled`), `sent_at`, `ics_uid`, `ics_sequence`, `gmail_thread_id` + `gmail_root_message_id` + `thread_connection_id` (the Sent thread lives in one mailbox, so a new sender starts a new thread), `created_by`, timestamps (M4). M6 adds `reminder_pending_hours` and `reminder_going_hours` (null = off; copied from the workspace defaults at creation), `last_nudged_at`, `last_nudged_count` and `cancelled_at`. Draft fields may be incomplete; `send_meeting` validates them.
- **`meeting_audience`** — (`meeting_id`, `list_id`) — which lists were picked.
- **`meeting_audience_people`** — (`meeting_id`, `contact_id`) PK, `mode` (`include` | `exclude`) — individuals ticked or unticked in the Audience step. People added through "Add people" become contacts immediately (`is_adhoc` when "Save to roster" is off) and are stored here as `include`.
- **`meeting_invitees`** — `id`, `meeting_id`, `contact_id` (unique per meeting), `token_hash` (set when the invite send starts, `dispatch_reserve`), `invited_at`, `email_status` (`queued` | `sent` | `skipped` | `failed` | `unknown`), `email_error`, `sent_at`. Snapshot taken at send time; "Invite more people" adds only contacts not yet invited. **M5 adds** `calendar_state` (`none` | `added`: whether the last calendar email sent to this person was an add or a removal), `calendar_sequence` (integer, raised on every calendar email to this person, used as the `.ics` `SEQUENCE`) and `calendar_requested_at` (announcement meetings: the person asked for a calendar invite).
- **`responses`** (M5) — `id`, `workspace_id`, `meeting_id`, `invitee_id` (unique), `status` (`attending` | `late` | `absent` | `not_attending`; RSVP uses `attending` / `not_attending`), `delay_minutes` (Late only; one of the meeting's `delay_options`), `reason` (≤ 500), `comment` (≤ 500; only when `comments_enabled`), `after_deadline` (saved after `response_deadline`), `needs_reconfirmation` (written from M6), `responded_at` (first answer), `updated_at`. Written only by `private.submit_response` (service role, via the token route).
- **`response_history`** (M5) — append-only: one row per save (`response_id`, `invitee_id`, `meeting_id`, `workspace_id`, the same answer fields, `after_deadline`, `changed_at`). Organizers read it; nobody updates or deletes it except cascades and account deletion (§11).
- **`meeting_changes`** (M6) — append-only audit log: `id`, `workspace_id`, `meeting_id`, `kind` (`edit` | `cancel`), `changes` (JSON: field → `[old, new]`), `notified` (whether people were emailed), `changed_by`, `changed_at`.
- **`attendance_marks`** (M6) — `invitee_id` PK (→ `meeting_invitees`, on delete cascade; per invited person rather than per contact, so a mark always belongs to someone who was invited, decided 2026-10-09), `workspace_id`, `meeting_id`, `actual` (`present` | `late` | `absent`), `late_minutes` (1–240, only with `late`; #257), `marked_by`, `marked_at` — check-in (actual vs declared).

### Integrations & pipeline
- **`google_connections`** — `id`, `user_id` (who connected it), `google_sub` (stable Google account id from the ID token), `google_email`, `granted_scopes`, `refresh_token_encrypted` (AES-256-GCM, versioned key `GOOGLE_TOKEN_ENCRYPTION_KEY`, connection id as associated data), `status` (`active` | `broken`), `broken_reason`, timestamps. Unique (`user_id`, `google_sub`): reconnecting the same account replaces the token. `authenticated` has no `SELECT` on the token column; only the dispatcher (service role) decrypts it.
- **`sheet_syncs`** (M7) — `workspace_id` (PK, on delete cascade), `connection_id` (→ `google_connections`, on delete set null: the account whose Drive holds the file), `google_sub` + `google_email` (that account, kept when the connection row is gone, for "Pick that account" and the resume rule), `create_key` (uuid tagging the file in Drive `appProperties`, so a file created just before a crash is found and reused, never duplicated), `spreadsheet_id` (null until the worker creates the file), `status` (`active` | `paused`), `paused_reason` (`trashed` | `deleted` | `access_lost` | `full`), `paused_at`, `alerted_at` (one platform email per pause), `synced_at`, `locked_until` + `run_id` (one worker run per sheet), `retry_at` + `attempts` (backoff after a Google error), `created_by`, timestamps. Only the state the screens and the worker need; written only through `private` functions.
- **`sheet_tabs`** (M7) — (`workspace_id` → `sheet_syncs` on delete cascade, `tab_key`) PK (`2026`, or `2026-1`, `2026-2` … newest first for a split year), `year`, `google_sheet_id` (Google's tab id, so a renamed tab is still found), `title`, `starts_from` / `starts_to` (the first and last meeting start it holds, so a meeting's Export link finds its tab), `content_hash` (of the title and rows written; an unchanged tab is skipped), `row_count`, `format_version` (formatting is re-applied when the code's `SHEET_FORMAT_VERSION` is newer, e.g. after a column is added), timestamps.
- **`sheet_dirty_years`** (M7) — (`workspace_id` → `sheet_syncs` on delete cascade, `year`) PK, `version` (raised on every change), `changed_at` (last change), `first_changed_at` (oldest change not yet written). Filled by statement-level triggers (§8); a year is cleared only if its `version` is still the one the worker read.
- **Access pattern (M7):** RLS on all three tables. `sheet_syncs` and `sheet_tabs`: SELECT for Owner/Admin/Viewer of the workspace (the link and tab ids; the account email is returned to the Owner only, by the route); no direct writes for `authenticated`. `sheet_dirty_years`: no access for `authenticated` (worker only). Writes are `private` `SECURITY DEFINER` functions behind `public` invoker wrappers (Owner checks inside); worker functions (`sheet_claim`, `sheet_finish`, `sheet_pause`, …) are executable by `service_role` only.
- **`push_subscriptions`** — `id`, `user_id`, `endpoint` (unique), `p256dh`, `auth`, timestamps.
- **`outbox_jobs`** — `id`, `kind` (`invite` | `calendar_confirm` | `update` | `cancel` | `reminder`; M7 rebuilt the enum without the never-used `sheet_sync`, `push` and `system_email`: M8 adds `push` back only if Web Push uses the outbox), `workspace_id`, `payload` (JSON validated by a per-kind Zod schema), `invitee_id` (per-person jobs; null for M6's per-meeting reminder timers), `run_id` (the claiming run), `send_started_at` (set when quota is reserved; an expired lease with it set means the outcome is unknown), `idempotency_key` (unique), `run_after`, `status` (`pending` | `processing` | `done` | `failed` | `paused`), `attempts`, `locked_until`, `last_error`, timestamps.
- **`send_log`** — one row per email reserved/sent (`google_sub`, `workspace_id`, `job_id`, `sent_at`), used for rolling-24 h quota checks **per Google account** (two workspaces sharing one Gmail share its budget).
- **`sender_leases`** — `google_sub` PK, `locked_until`: one dispatcher run owns a sender at a time (pacing, threading order).
- **Rate limiting** — `private.rate_limit_events` + `private.hit_rate_limit` / `hit_user_rate_limit` with limits in `private.app_limits` (M2/M3). M4 adds `meetings_per_user_per_hour`, `gmail_sends_per_day` (400), `gmail_sends_per_minute` (60), `invitees_per_meeting_max` (500).
- **`abuse_reports`** — `id`, `workspace_id`, `invitee_id`, `reported_at` — "Not my group" clicks (M4); surfaced to platform admins (M9).
- **`workspace_sending_suspensions`** (M9) — `workspace_id`, `reason` (`auto_reports` | `platform_admin`), `created_at`, `lifted_at`. Automatic suspension and lifting ship together with the platform admin page.
- **Access pattern (M4):** RLS + explicit `GRANT`s; reads for Owner/Admin/Viewer of the workspace; draft field edits and draft deletes for Owner/Admin through RLS; creating a meeting (`create_meeting`, rate limited) and every audience write (`set_meeting_audience`, `add_meeting_people`, capped per meeting) only through functions, and a one-off guest joining the roster is counted against the contacts cap by a trigger on every path (security review 2026-10-07); anything touching the queue (`send_meeting`, `invite_more`, `set_workspace_sender`, sender resume) through `private` `SECURITY DEFINER` functions behind `public` wrappers; queue internals (`claim_outbox_jobs`, `reserve_send`, `complete_job`, token-page functions) executable by `service_role` only.
- **Access pattern (M5):** `responses` and `response_history` — RLS SELECT for Owner/Admin/Viewer of the workspace, no INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE for `authenticated`. Members write only through `public.token_submit_response(token_hash, …)` and `public.token_request_calendar(token_hash)` (executable by the service role only, like the M4 `token_*` functions; called by the token routes), which validate everything, write the history row and refresh the calendar job in one transaction. Organizer reads go through functions that return one page or one aggregate (`meetings_page`, `meeting_results`, `meeting_people`, `contact_history`, `attendance_summary`, `attendance_details`): `private` definer bodies behind `public` invoker wrappers that check membership once, written with the M4 `import_contacts` lesson: read each table once, join aggregates instead of per-row subqueries or per-row `is_member()` probes, and check `EXPLAIN` with fresh and stale statistics.

- **Access pattern (M6):** `meeting_changes` and `attendance_marks` — RLS SELECT for Owner/Admin/Viewer of the workspace, no direct writes for `authenticated`. Every M6 write is a `private` `SECURITY DEFINER` function behind a `public` invoker wrapper: `edit_sent_meeting` (Owner/Admin, before the start), `cancel_meeting`, `delete_cancelled_meeting`, `nudge_meeting` (12 h per meeting, `nudge_interval_hours` in `app_limits`), `mark_attendance` and `mark_rest_as_declared` (Owner/Admin, or Viewer with `can_check_in`; after the start; answer meetings only), `duplicate_meeting` (counts against `meetings_per_user_per_hour`). The answer-deadline rule (now < deadline < start) lives in one SQL predicate, `private.response_deadline_problem`, called by `send_meeting` (first send only) and `edit_sent_meeting`; its TypeScript twin is `responseDeadlineProblem` (`src/lib/meetings/deadline.ts`, #220).

### Identity rules
- Personal link tokens: 256 bits, **derived** as HMAC-SHA256(`INVITE_TOKEN_SECRET`, invitee id), base64url; only the SHA-256 hash is stored. The dispatcher recomputes the link for any later email (updates, reminders) without storing raw tokens; rotating the secret invalidates every link at once (emergency switch). The same token serves `/r/`, `/u/` (unsubscribe) and `/report/`.
- A signed-in user sees invites whose contact email equals their **verified** auth email, across all workspaces.
- Personal links are bearer links (forwarding lets the recipient answer). Accepted risk; the page shows "Answering as <Full name>. Not you?" with an explanation.
- Workspaces are private: no directory or public join page; access only via organizer invite or a member's email link.

## 7. Core Flows

### 7.1 Organizer onboarding
Sign in → `/welcome` asks "What should we call you?" once if the profile has no name → 0 workspaces: create workspace (name, timezone pre-filled from the browser); otherwise the last opened workspace (`profiles.last_workspace_id`), with a switcher in the header. An invite link carries a same-site return path through sign-in. Home shows a checklist: import members, connect Gmail sending (required before the first send), connect Google Sheets (optional), invite committee as Viewers.

### 7.2 Create & send a meeting (center "+", M4)
"+" (Owner/Admin) creates a draft and opens `/w/[slug]/meetings/[id]/edit?step=details`; the step lives in the URL so Back and reload work, and every step change saves the draft (drafts are listed under Meetings > Drafts and can be finished on another device). A draft left with no title and no date is hidden from Drafts and deleted by the daily housekeeping after 24 h; each draft card has a "…" menu with Delete (confirm dialog) (decided 2026-10-07). Footer buttons have one fixed height across steps.
1. **Details** — Title; **When**: a date button opening a month-calendar popover and a time button opening a 15-minute list (typing "1830" jumps), in the workspace time zone with "Change"; **Duration** chips (30 min, 1 h, 1 h 30, 2 h, Other; default from Meeting defaults); **Where**: segmented In person / Online / Both. In person asks for the place. Online asks "Where online?" in words (e.g. "Club Discord, Meetings voice channel") and an optional link; at least one of the two is needed. Both asks for the place and the online part. Online fields start from the workspace's usual online place (Settings > Meeting defaults). A link to Discord, Google Meet, Zoom or Microsoft Teams shows "Join on <app>" in the email and pages, any other link "Join online" (owner decision 2026-10-08); **Agenda** (optional Markdown, Write / Preview with a small toolbar: bold, italic, list, link).
2. **Audience** — list chips with counts at the top (tap to include a whole list); a count bar ("28 people · 2 in both lists"); below, everyone included, searchable, each with a checkbox to untick and their list tag; unsubscribed or reported people are shown disabled with the reason; an **Extra people** card with **Add people**, a sheet for **several** non-roster people at once (repeatable Full name + Email rows, "+ Another person", "Paste a list" with one `name, email` per line) and a **Save to roster** switch that starts on (off = one-off guests, `is_adhoc`). Adding an email already in the roster merges into that contact. The Next button names the deduplicated total.
3. **Responses** — mode (announcement / rsvp / attendance) and its settings (delay options, reason required, comments, footer note), optional answer deadline (later than now and before the start; the same day is fine; each error shows on the date or time box to change, #220); pre-filled from Meeting defaults, overridable per meeting. From M6, two reminder rows (§7.6), hidden for announcements.
4. **Review** — one summary card per step with **Edit**; an email preview card opening the full rendered email (example recipient); the sender line ("From **GDG ISSAT** <club@gmail.com>") and the quota line ("30 emails now · 370 left today on this Gmail"); **Save draft** and **Send N invites**. Send opens a confirm dialog: "Send 30 invites from club@gmail.com now?".

Without a sender: the Owner sees **Connect Gmail to send** on Review (the OAuth round trip returns to the draft); an Admin sees "Ask <Owner name> to connect Gmail" and can only save the draft, and the Owner gets a Home "needs attention" item.

Confirming calls `send_meeting`: snapshot of invitees (lists ∪ included − excluded − unsubscribed), one `invite` job each, `status = scheduled`, then an immediate dispatch via `after()`. The page moves to the **meeting page** (`/meetings/[id]`): details, a send-progress card ("Sending invites 12 / 30 · you can leave this page"; over quota: "18 queued, resumes about 14:20"; no sender: "Paused: connect Gmail to send"), and the invitee list with a status per person (Queued / Sent / Skipped / Failed with reason / Delivery unknown). Bounces cannot be seen (`gmail.send` cannot read the inbox): the page says that bounce notices arrive in the sender's Gmail. **Invite more people** reopens the Audience step showing who is already invited, then the same confirm dialog; only new people are emailed. M4 is proven with test addresses only; the first real club send waits for the response page (M5).

### 7.3 Member responds (no account, M5)
The email shows the meeting card and the answer buttons (deep links to `/r/{token}?choice=…`) — **no `.ics`**, so Gmail shows no competing RSVP buttons. Announcement emails get one button, "Add to my calendar", linking to `/r/{token}`.

**Page `/r/[token]`** (layout "choice cards", decided 2026-10-08; phone first):
- **Meeting card:** workspace sticker and "<Workspace> invites you", title, when (meeting time zone, plus "your time: …" when the browser's zone differs, §7.10), where with the platform-named Join button, agenda folded.
- **"Are you coming?"** with one large card per choice: attendance "I'm going" / "I'll be late" / "I can't come"; RSVP "Going" / "Not going". `?choice=` (`attending|late|absent|going|not_going`) **only pre-selects** that card; a value that does not fit the meeting's mode is ignored. Opening the page never saves anything (link scanners).
- **Form inside the selected card:** Late shows the meeting's delay chips (one required). Late and Absent (RSVP: Not going) show the reason box — required when `reason_required`, optional otherwise, ≤ 500 characters. A comment box (≤ 500) is shown for every answer when `comments_enabled`. The `footer_note` sits under the form.
- **Confirm** names the answer ("Confirm: late by 20 min"). Saving shows the CONFIRMED stamp dropping in with a small shake, plus confetti for Going (reduced motion: a plain fade), then "Your answer: Late by 20 min" with **Change** and, for Going/Late, "We emailed you a calendar invite".
- **Footer:** "Answering as <Full name>. Not you?" (opens a short explanation: the link is personal; ask the organizers for your own invite) and "Your answer is visible to <Workspace> organizers".
- **States:** after the deadline — a notice ("The answer deadline has passed. You can still answer.") and the answer is flagged `after_deadline`; after the meeting starts — the answer read-only, "Answers are closed"; announcement — the meeting card and an **Email me a calendar invite** button (sets `calendar_requested_at`; afterwards "We emailed you a calendar invite"); unsubscribed — answering works, no calendar email is sent, and a note offers "Subscribe again" (`/u/[token]`); time changed (M6) — "The time changed to Sat 10 Oct, 18:00. You said: Going." with **Yes, still going** (one tap saves the same answer and clears the flag) above the choice cards; cancelled (M6) — "This meeting was cancelled" with the meeting card; unknown or deleted token — the neutral "This link isn't valid" page.
- **Calendar:** every save refreshes the person's single pending `calendar_confirm` job (§8), which adds the event for Going/Late and removes it after a switch to Absent / Not going.
- Each save marks the meeting's year as changed for the workspace's Google Sheet, when one exists (M7, §8).

### 7.4 Member with an account
`/invites` lists upcoming invites across workspaces with their status and one-tap change. Can enable push notifications, later calendar auto-add (layer B), export or delete data.

### 7.5 Edit / cancel / delete (M6)
- **Entry:** the meeting page's "…" menu (Owner/Admin): **Edit** and **Cancel meeting** until the start, **Duplicate** always, **Delete** for cancelled meetings. "Invite more people" stays as in M4.
- **Edit:** `?mode=edit` runs Details → Answers → **Review changes**. Edits are held on the device (session storage per meeting) until saved, because saving emails people. Answers shows the answer type and delay chips as fixed; reason, comments, note, deadline and reminder settings stay editable. Review changes lists each change (old → new), who will be emailed and the quota line; text-only changes show "Email everyone about this change" (off by default). **Save and email N people** opens a confirm dialog.
- **`edit_sent_meeting`** (one transaction, row lock): checks role and that the meeting has not started; validates the fields and the deadline (`private.response_deadline_problem`); writes `meeting_changes`; raises `ics_sequence`; if `starts_at` changed, sets `needs_reconfirmation` on every response; if `starts_at`, the deadline or a reminder setting changed, moves the reminder timers; replaces each recipient's pending `update` job (one per person, `run_after = now() + calendar_confirm_delay`, so several quick edits send one email).
- **Recipients:** invite `sent` or `unknown`, not unsubscribed. Time or duration change → all of them; place change → all except `absent` / `not_attending`; text-only → only with the switch on. Calendar holders (`calendar_state = 'added'`) whose event's content changed get an update job even when nobody else is emailed (then the email is only the calendar update).
- **Update email:** "<Workspace> changed <Title>" with what changed (old → new) and the meeting card; after a time change, the answer buttons ("Still going?"); otherwise "Change my answer". For a calendar holder still Going/Late, the same email carries a pre-accepted `REQUEST` with the new `SEQUENCE`.
- **Results:** while anyone is to reconfirm, a **To reconfirm** tile appears and those people leave the Going/Late/Absent tiles; History keeps their latest answer.
- **Cancel** (`cancel_meeting`, confirm "Cancel this meeting and email N people?"): `status = cancelled`, `cancelled_at`, `meeting_changes` row; pending `invite`, `update`, `reminder` and `calendar_confirm` jobs of the meeting are marked done unsent (unsent invitees become `skipped`); one `cancel` job per invitee whose invite went out (not unsubscribed): a short cancellation email, with `METHOD:CANCEL` inside for calendar holders.
- **Cancelled meetings:** listed with a **Cancelled** label; answers viewable and exportable; excluded from History, Attendance and Home counts; the answer page shows the cancelled state. No un-cancel.
- **Delete** (`delete_cancelled_meeting`, confirm "Delete for good? Its answers will be gone."): only when cancelled and no `cancel` job is still pending; deletes the meeting (cascades: invitees, responses, history, marks, changes, jobs).
- **Invite more after the deadline** is allowed: the deadline only blocks a draft's first Send (#220 finding).

### 7.6 Reminders and nudge (M6)
- **Settings:** Settings > Meeting defaults and the wizard's Answers step: "People who haven't answered" (Off, 1, 2, 6, 24, 48 h before the deadline, or before the start without one; default 24 h) and "Going and Late" (Off, 1, 2, 6, 24 h before the start; default 2 h). Hidden for announcements.
- **Timers:** `send_meeting` (first send) enqueues up to two per-meeting `reminder` jobs (`invitee_id` null, payload `{meeting, audience: pending | going}`, `run_after` = due time); a due time already past is skipped. `edit_sent_meeting` moves them (or creates one whose new due time is in the future); `cancel_meeting` drops them; Invite more needs nothing.
- **Fan-out:** when a timer runs, one set-based insert queues a per-person `reminder` job for each eligible invitee — *pending*: invite `sent`/`unknown`, not unsubscribed, no response or `needs_reconfirmation`; *going*: response `attending`/`late` without `needs_reconfirmation` — and the timer is done. Each per-person job re-checks eligibility at reserve time (answered meanwhile → done, nothing sent) and fails as "meeting started" like invites, so a reminder delayed by the quota never arrives after the start.
- **Nudge:** before the start, the meeting page shows **Remind N who haven't answered** (Owner/Admin) → confirm "Email N people who haven't answered from <sender>?" → `nudge_meeting` queues per-person *pending* reminders and sets `last_nudged_at`/`last_nudged_count`; then "Reminded N at 14:20 · available again at 02:20" (`nudge_interval_hours` 12).
- **Emails:** *pending* — "Reminder: please answer by <deadline>" (or "Please answer" after it) with the answer buttons; *going* — "See you at 18:00 today" with the place or Join button and "Change my answer". Same quota, pacing, thread and pausing as invites.

### 7.7 Viewer (committee)
Read-only Home, meetings, responses, per-member history ("Late 3×, Absent 1×, No reply 4× this semester" + reasons; declared vs actual where check-in exists, M6), CSV/Excel export, link to synced Sheet (M7). Viewers with `can_check_in` can mark actual attendance (M6). Owners and Admins see the same views (M5, decided 2026-10-08):
- **Meeting page (`/meetings/[id]`):** header; one email line ("Emails: 29 sent · 1 not delivered ▸", opening M4's per-person delivery list; while invites are still going out, M4's progress card stays); **count tiles** — attendance: Going / Late / Absent / No reply; RSVP: Going / Not going / No reply; announcement: no tiles, "N asked for a calendar invite". **No reply** = the invite email went out (`sent` or `unknown`) and the person has not answered; failed or skipped emails count under "not delivered". Tapping a tile filters the list below, tapping it again shows everyone. Rows: name, answer pill, delay, reason, comment, "after the deadline", answered at; pages of 50 with "Show more"; a table from `md` up; a name opens the person sheet. The page refreshes every 10 s while visible (TanStack `refetchInterval`, paused in background tabs) and stops a few hours after the meeting ends.
- **Meetings list and Home:** each sent meeting's card shows "17 going · 4 late · 6 no reply"; Home's next-meeting card shows the same live counts.
- **Person sheet** (Lists, or a name on a meeting page): a **History** part with period chips (Last 30 days, Last 3 months (default), This year, All time, From–to with the date picker), four counts (Going, Late, Absent, No reply) and the past meetings in pages with answer, delay and reason. Only meetings that have started, asked for answers and were not cancelled count (announcements do not); RSVP Not going counts as Absent. From M6 the counts use the check-in where one exists, else the answer (Present counts as Going), and a row shows the check-in next to the answer whenever there is one: "Going · Was present", "Said going · Was absent", "Said late by 10 min · Was late by 15 min" (owner, 2026-10-10, #257).
- **Lists → Attendance:** a People / Attendance switch on the Lists page; the Attendance table (name, Going, Late, Absent, No reply; sortable) uses the same list chips and period chips; a row opens the person sheet. Loaded at once and virtualized, like the roster (§4 Pagination).
- **Export** (header menu, everyone who can see the data): meeting answers as CSV or Excel (name, email, lists, answer, delay, reason, comment, answered at in the meeting time zone, after deadline, email status); Attendance as CSV (summary) or Excel (sheets Summary and Details: one row per person per meeting). Files are built on the device; CSV starts with a UTF-8 BOM, and CSV cells starting with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, tab or carriage return are prefixed with `'` (formula injection); Excel cells are written as text, which spreadsheets never evaluate. M6 adds **Checked in**, **Checked in by** and **Was late by (min)** to the meeting export and the Attendance Details sheet; the Summary uses the same counts as the screen. **Excel look (owner, 2026-10-10, #257):** every sheet opens with a centred title band (what it is, the workspace, the meeting time or the period, the export time), then a bold header filled with the app's primary (Summary's Going / Late / Absent / No reply headers in their tile colours); answer and check-in cells are tinted like the tiles; table cells have thin ink borders; the title and header rows stay in place while scrolling; grid lines stay; widths fit the content; Arial (ships with every spreadsheet app). CSV stays plain.

### 7.8 Post-meeting check-in (M6)
- **Who and when:** Owners, Admins and Viewers with `can_check_in` (Settings > People), from the start with no end, for meetings that ask for answers and are not cancelled.
- **Screen:** after the start, a **Results / Check-in** switch on the meeting page (only for those allowed). The check-in list is paged (50, keyset) with search; each row has Present / Late / Absent chips; a tap saves at once (optimistic, rolled back with a row error on failure); unmarked rows show the declared answer as a dashed hint (Going → Present, Late → Late, Can't come → Absent, No reply → none). Late opens "How late?" under the name: the meeting's delay choices and **Other** (a minutes field, 1–240, saved on Enter or leaving the field); optional; pre-picked from the declared delay (#257). **Mark the rest as they said (N)** (confirm dialog) marks every unmarked person from their answer, No reply → Absent; a Late answer keeps its minutes. A count line: "18 of 30 checked in".
- **Data:** `attendance_marks` written only by `mark_attendance` / `mark_rest_as_declared`; History, Attendance and exports as in §7.7.

### 7.9 Duplicate meeting (M6)
From the meeting page's "…" menu and each Meetings card's "…" menu: `duplicate_meeting` copies the title, details, lists, included/excluded people, answer settings and reminder settings into a new draft with an empty date, time and deadline, and opens the wizard on Details. Counts against `meetings_per_user_per_hour`.

### 7.10 Timezones
Times are always shown in the meeting's timezone, plus "(your time: …)" when the viewer's timezone differs. Windows browsers in Tunisia report `Africa/Lagos` (same UTC+1, no DST): kept as reported, fixed per workspace in Settings > General; no automatic suggestion (owner decision 2026-10-08).

### 7.11 Workspace lifecycle
Exactly one Owner always exists. The Owner transfers ownership to an existing Admin (instant and atomic, confirmed by typing the workspace name; the old Owner becomes Admin) and must do so before leaving. Only the Owner deletes a workspace: typing its name confirms, and all its data is hard-deleted (synced Sheets remain in Drive).


### 7.12 Abuse controls
- Meeting emails come from each workspace's own Gmail, so abuse affects that sender's own quota and reputation, not the platform.
- **M4:** "Not my group" records an `abuse_reports` row and unsubscribes the reporter from that workspace; Admins see "Reported: not my group" on that person (roster and Audience step).
- **M9:** reports are visible to platform admins; automatic suspension (threshold on reports) and manual suspend/lift ship together with the internal admin page.

### 7.13 Viewer and Admin invites
Admins invite Viewers; only the Owner invites Admins. Several addresses can be entered at once (chips: comma, space, Enter or a pasted list; up to 20 per request): each address still gets its own email-bound invite and token, and the dialog lists the result per person with "Copy link" per row and "Copy all links". Delivery: email from the platform sender or "Copy link" (the token is shown once; copying an existing invite renews it). `/invite/[token]` requires sign-in first (no signed-out invite lookup), then shows workspace, role and masked email; accepting requires the signed-in **verified** email to equal the invite email. States: ready, wrong account (with "Switch account"), expired, revoked, used, already a member. The preview shows the workspace name in the expired, revoked and used states too: accepted (decided 2026-10-06, #87), because only the holder of the 256-bit token can ask and the invite was addressed to them.

### 7.14 Roster and lists (M3)
- **Roster page** (`/w/[slug]/lists`): search ("Search N people", name and email, accent-insensitive), a scrolling row of list chips ("All N", one per list with its count, "Manage" to create/rename/delete lists), then the people. Phones: virtualized cards (name, email, list chips); tapping one opens a bottom-sheet editor whose fields save on blur, with a list picker (create a list by typing a new name) and Delete. From `md` up: an inline-editable grid (select, Full name, Email, Lists) with keyboard navigation. Owner/Admin only: Import, "+ Add", Select mode (sticky action bar: add to list, remove from list, delete N). Viewers see the same data with no edit controls.
- **Edits:** optimistic, rolled back with an inline error on failure (e.g. "Already in your roster: <name>" for a taken email). Deletes show an Undo toast and are sent only after it expires (about 5 s); closing the tab before then cancels the delete.
- **Import dialog** (full screen on phones), three steps with footer buttons of one fixed height and single-line labels: (1) **Source** — File (`.csv`/`.xlsx`, ≤ 5 MB, parsed on the device; sheet picker for multi-sheet workbooks) or Paste (tab-separated text from Sheets/Excel); (2) **Match columns** — each header auto-matched to Full name / Email / Lists / Ignore from English and French synonyms, with sample values, a "First row is headers" switch and an optional "Also add everyone to…" list (which can be created there); (3) **Preview** — the server's dry run: count tiles (new, updated, unchanged, merged duplicates, invalid — tapping filters the rows), new lists, and each row with its outcome and reason; invalid rows are skipped; over a limit, Import is disabled with an explanation. Nothing is saved before "Import N people". The file never leaves the device; only `{full_name, email, lists[]}` rows are sent.
- **Empty roster:** "Import your roster", "Paste from Sheets", "Add one person". The Home checklist item "Import members" links here.

### 7.15 Workspace sender Gmail (M4)

## 8. Background Pipeline

- **Dispatcher (M4):** `POST /api/internal/dispatch`, `Authorization: Bearer DISPATCH_SECRET` (constant-time compare). Called (a) right after `send_meeting` / `invite_more` via `after()`, and (b) every minute by Supabase Cron on production: `private.kick_dispatcher()` calls `net.http_post` **only when jobs are due** (pending with `run_after <= now()`, or an expired lease); the URL and secret live in Supabase Vault. Preview projects have no dispatcher cron (§5).
- **Claiming:** `claim_outbox_jobs` takes a lease on one sender (`sender_leases`, per Google account) and claims that sender's due jobs with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, setting `status = processing`, `locked_until` and `attempts + 1`. One run owns a sender at a time, so pacing and thread order hold across concurrent runs; expired leases are reclaimable. Sends are paced at **one per second per sender**; each run stops after ~50 s and the next tick continues (500 invites take about 9 minutes).
- **Per job, at run time:** contact unsubscribed since the snapshot → `skipped`; meeting already started → `failed` ("meeting started"); workspace has no active sender → job `paused` (resumed when the Owner connects or reconnects); otherwise `reserve_send` inserts the `send_log` row only if the sender's rolling-24 h count is under the cap (advisory lock per Google account), then the email is sent and `complete_job` records the result (invitee status, `token_hash`, job `done`) in one transaction.
- **Threading:** the first email of a meeting is sent with our own `Message-ID` (`gmail_root_message_id`) and no `threadId`; Gmail's returned `threadId` is stored with `thread_connection_id`. Later emails pass `threadId` plus `In-Reply-To`/`References` = the root id. Verified 2026-10-07: with `threadId` set, emails to different recipients (even with a changed subject) show as **one conversation** in the sender's Sent folder; headers alone (no `threadId`) do not thread; recipients see an ordinary email (no "Re:", nothing quoted). Gmail reportedly splits conversations after about 100 messages (third-party source), so large meetings may show a few rows. If a threaded send is rejected, the dispatcher starts a new root.
- **Senders** — `EmailSender` interface:
  - `GmailApiSender` — the workspace's sender Gmail via the Gmail REST API (`users.messages.send` with plain `fetch`; the raw RFC 822 message is built with nodemailer's MIME composer, which handles UTF-8 names and subjects; no `googleapis` dependency). Access tokens are refreshed from the decrypted refresh token once per run per sender.
  - No fallback sender for meeting emails. A workspace without a connected sender cannot dispatch; jobs wait in `paused` with a "Connect Gmail to send" prompt.
  - System emails (sign-in codes, Viewer invites) do not go through this pipeline: Supabase Auth sends codes via its custom SMTP setting; Viewer invite emails use a small `SystemMailer` (nodemailer, same SMTP credentials), sent directly from the route. Switching to Resend later is a configuration change.
  - **Platform Gmail budget** (Gmail blocks the account for 1–24 h above 500 emails/day): Supabase auth email rate limit **15/hour** (≤ 360/day) + Viewer invite emails capped at **100/day platform-wide and 20/day per workspace** (Postgres rate limits, values in config) → worst case 460/day. The M4 "Gmail sending needs reconnecting" alert (one per breakage) shares the Viewer-invite platform cap. Over a cap, the UI offers "Copy link instead".
- **Quotas (rolling 24 h, values in `private.app_limits`):**
  - Gmail limits (checked 2026-10-07): a regular Gmail account is blocked for 1–24 h above **500 emails/day** or 500 recipients in one email ([Gmail Help](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/22839)); `messages.send` costs 100 of **6,000 quota units per minute per user** ([Gmail API limits](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/reference/quota)) → ≤ 60 sends/min.
  - TapNShow caps each Google account at **400 emails per rolling 24 h** (`gmail_sends_per_day`), leaving ~100 for the person's own mail that we cannot count, and **60/min** (`gmail_sends_per_minute`; pacing keeps it at 1/s anyway). One email per recipient (no BCC: every member has a personal link).
  - Over the cap → the job's `run_after` moves to when the oldest send in the window expires; the meeting page shows "N queued, resumes about HH:MM". Review shows "N emails now · M left today on this Gmail".
- **Errors:**

  | Gmail result | Handling |
  |---|---|
  | No HTTP response (network error, timeout) after the request was sent | `unknown`: **not retried** ("Delivery unknown, check Sent") — the email may have gone out |
  | 429, 5xx | Retry after 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 min (reservation released), then `failed` |
  | 400 invalid recipient | `failed` for that invitee with a readable reason |
  | Daily / user rate limit exceeded | All of that sender's jobs pushed back 1 h |
  | `invalid_grant` | Connection `broken`, that sender's jobs `paused`, Owner alerted (Home, meeting banner, one platform email per breakage) |
  | Bounces | Not visible: `gmail.send` cannot read the inbox; bounce notices land in the sender's Gmail, and the meeting page says so |

- **Idempotency — at most once:** unique `idempotency_key` (`invite:{invitee}`); a duplicate enqueue is a no-op; the reservation, the send and `complete_job` cover every path except a crash between Gmail accepting the email and `complete_job`, which leaves the invitee `unknown` rather than retrying ("no member is ever emailed twice" wins over "every member is emailed").
- **Housekeeping (daily `pg_cron`, M4):** delete `private.rate_limit_events` older than 2 days, `done`/`failed` jobs and `send_log` rows older than 30 days, stale sender leases, and drafts with no title and no date older than 24 hours. The M4 migration declares `pg_cron` and `pg_net` (enabled by hand during S1) and moves `pg_net` out of `public` (`drop extension pg_net; create extension pg_net schema extensions;` with an empty `net.http_request_queue`, per Supabase's troubleshooting guide).
- **`calendar_confirm` (M5) — final-state job, one pending per person** (decided 2026-10-08):
  - Every answer save (and the announcement "Email me a calendar invite") refreshes the invitee's pending (or paused) `calendar_confirm` job to `run_after = now() + calendar_confirm_delay` (60 s, `app_limits`) or inserts one, under the invitee row lock, so saves never create two; a job retried after a later save may leave a duplicate, which is harmless because every job re-decides at run time. Each job has a fresh `idempotency_key` (`calendar:{invitee}:{uuid}`).
  - At run time the dispatcher computes **wanted** = (answer is `attending` or `late`, or announcement with `calendar_requested_at`) and the meeting has not started and the contact is not unsubscribed. wanted and `calendar_state = 'none'` → `METHOD:REQUEST` (add); not wanted and `calendar_state = 'added'` → `METHOD:CANCEL` (remove); otherwise the job is `done` with nothing sent (quick flips, a Late delay change).
  - Sent through the same path as invites: sender lease, `reserve_send` against the per-Gmail cap, paused without an active sender, threaded into the meeting's Sent thread. `complete_job` sets `calendar_state` and `calendar_sequence + 1` in the same transaction. **Unknown outcome** (no HTTP response) is never retried and is recorded as if sent: the worst case is a later removal for an event that never arrived, which is harmless.
- **#168 fixes (M5):** on the daily cap, defer all of that sender's due jobs to the window's reopening (no re-claim loop); do not start a send when less than the Gmail timeout plus a margin is left before the run's `maxDuration`; build wall times from numbers (`new TZDate(y, m - 1, d, h, min, zone)`) so a spring-forward gap in the browser's zone cannot shift a meeting.
- **`update` and `cancel` (M6):** per invitee, sent through the same path as invites (lease, `reserve_send`, pausing, thread). `update` is final-state like `calendar_confirm`: at most one pending per person, replaced by a newer edit; at run time it renders the meeting as it is then, adds the pre-accepted `REQUEST` (new `SEQUENCE`) for a calendar holder still Going/Late, and is done unsent when nothing is left to say. `cancel` carries `METHOD:CANCEL` for calendar holders. `complete_job` updates `calendar_state`/`calendar_sequence` as for calendar emails.
- **`reminder` (M6):** per-meeting timers fan out into per-person jobs when due (§7.6); per-person jobs re-check eligibility at reserve time; manual nudges insert per-person jobs directly. Idempotency keys: `reminder:{meeting}:{audience}:{uuid}` (timers), `reminder:{invitee}:{timer or nudge id}` (people).
- **Google Sheet worker (M7):**
  - **Changes:** statement-level `AFTER` triggers (transition tables, so one statement costs one write however many rows it touches) upsert `sheet_dirty_years` for the years whose rows change, only for workspaces with a `sheet_syncs` row (one index probe otherwise): meetings (title, start, time zone, status, delete; a move across years marks both), invitees (added, removed, email status), responses, attendance marks, contacts (name, email, delete), list memberships, and — marking every year that has a tab — list names and the workspace name. Housekeeping marks the current year once a day so manual edits are repaired even without changes. Trigger functions compare the columns the sheet shows, so unrelated updates (e.g. `unsubscribed_at`, calendar state) mark nothing; cascaded deletes (a contact or meeting) fire the child tables' triggers too. Paused sheets keep being marked, so a resume catches up in one pass. When a sheet is created (first connect or Create a new sheet) every year with a meeting is marked.
  - **Waking:** `private.kick_dispatcher()` calls the internal route when emails are due **or** a sheet is due; the route runs the two workers side by side in one `after()` (`Promise.allSettled`, so one failing never stops the other), each with its own budget inside the route's `maxDuration` (60 s), and a worker with nothing to do returns after one RPC. The secret check is one shared helper.
  - **Claim:** `sheet_claim` leases up to 5 (`SHEET_CLAIM_BATCH`) active sheets (`FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`) whose lease is free, whose `retry_at` has passed, and that have a changed year with `changed_at` ≥ 1 min ago or `first_changed_at` ≥ 5 min ago (constant changes cannot starve a sheet), plus sheets with no file yet; a partial index serves it. Leases last 120 s, twice the route's `maxDuration`, so a run is always dead before its lease can be taken over and two runs never write one sheet.
  - **Per sheet:** refresh the access token (`google-oauth`, shared with Gmail; `invalid_grant` or a missing `drive.file` → paused `access_lost`); create the file when there is none: first look for one tagged with this sheet's `create_key` (Drive `files.list` on `appProperties`, which `drive.file` covers for the app's own files), else Drive `files.create` (`mimeType` `application/vnd.google-apps.spreadsheet`, `appProperties` `{tapnshow_workspace, tapnshow_create_key}`), and save its id at once; its first tab becomes the current year's; otherwise `files.get?fields=trashed` (trashed → paused `trashed`; 404 → paused `deleted`); changed years go newest first and carry on in later runs when the budget ends (a first fill of years of history takes a few runs); before writing, the projected cell count is checked against `SHEET_CELLS_MAX` (19 million, under Google's 20 million) and a sheet that would pass it is paused `full`; for each changed year read its rows in one set-based query (labels in the workspace's locale), split them into tabs, and skip a tab whose `content_hash` is unchanged; otherwise one `batchUpdate` per tab writes the values only (text as `stringValue`, so `=…` never runs as a formula; minutes as numbers) and sets the exact row count (leftover rows go). Colours come from three hidden helper columns written with the values (the meeting band `odd`/`even`/`cancelled`, the Answer tone and the Checked in tone, as `FillTone` names from `ANSWER_TONE` / `ACTUAL_TONE`) and conditional-format rules on them (tone rules first, then band rules), so they never depend on translated words. Each redraw also sets the exact row count, the borders and fonts of the data rows and the column widths (fitted like the Excel export). The rest of the formatting (title band merges, header, frozen rows, hidden helper columns, the conditional-format rules over open-ended ranges, the slicer) is applied when a tab is created, when its `format_version` is older than the code's, and when a small metadata read shows it missing. Finally each written year is deleted from `sheet_dirty_years` only if its `version` is unchanged, so a change during a write is redrawn by the next run.
  - **Errors:** 429 and 5xx → `retry_at` with exponential backoff and jitter from 1 min up to 30 min (Sheets usage limits, checked 2026-10-10: 60 write and 60 read requests per minute per user per project, 300 per project, no daily limit, free; Google plans to charge for exceeding them, so the coalescing keeps each sheet at about one write per minute); 401 → one token refresh, then `access_lost`; 403 with a rate-limit reason (`rateLimitExceeded`, `userRateLimitExceeded`; Drive reports limits as 403) → retry; 403 because an API is not enabled (`accessNotConfigured`, `SERVICE_DISABLED`) → retry and a Sentry error (our setup, not the Owner's); any other 403 → `access_lost`; 404 → `deleted`; 400 (a bug on our side) → retry and a Sentry error, never a pause. A network failure or timeout is retried: a redraw is idempotent, so a write that did land is simply written again, and rows are never duplicated. Each Google request has a 15 s timeout and is started only with at least 20 s left of the worker's 50 s budget; claiming stops at the same point.
  - **Resume:** every successful Google callback (sending or sheets) re-checks the sheets on that Google account (`google_sub`): with `drive.file` granted, `access_lost` sheets move to the new connection and become active; without it, active ones pause `access_lost`. Try again sets `retry_at` to now; a sheet paused `trashed` or `deleted` resumes when the next run finds the file back. Turning off during a run is harmless: the run's final updates find no row and stop.
  - **Logs:** counts and ids only (rows written, tabs, bytes, ms), never names, emails or reasons; Google tokens are scrubbed (§11).
  - **Sizes (estimates, measured in the M7 plan):** about 200 bytes per row, so a 10,000-row tab is about 2 MB, the recommended maximum payload per request; requests time out at 180 s; a spreadsheet holds 20 million cells (Google Drive Help, checked 2026-10-10). The club writes about 1,500 rows a year; the extreme (2,000 people weekly) about 100,000.
- **Web Push (M8):** delete subscriptions on 404/410; whether it uses the outbox is decided in the M8 plan.

## 9. Integrations & Auth

- **Sign-in:** Supabase Auth — Google provider (`openid email profile`) and email OTP. Supabase custom SMTP points to the dedicated platform Gmail (`smtp.gmail.com:587`, app password, ~500 recipients/day) until a domain is bought, then Resend (free 3,000/month). The built-in mailer is unusable (2/hour, team-members only). Rate limited per address (Supabase: one code request per 60 s) and per IP (our Postgres limit).
  - **Email code:** `POST /api/auth/otp/send` → `signInWithOtp`; `POST /api/auth/otp/verify` → `verifyOtp`. Codes: 8 digits, 15-minute expiry. The code is in both the "Magic Link" and "Confirm signup" templates.
  - **Google:** own flow on our domain because Supabase's built-in flow shows `<ref>.supabase.co` on Google's consent screen and only a paid custom domain changes that. `GET /api/auth/google/start` (state + PKCE + nonce in a short-lived httpOnly cookie) → Google → `GET /api/auth/google/callback` (verify state, exchange code with the client secret, `signInWithIdToken`). OAuth client `tapnshow-web` (GCP project `tapnshow`); redirect URIs for production and `localhost:3000` only, so Google sign-in is unavailable on preview deployments (email codes work there). Supabase's Google provider holds the client ID. Brand verification (app name + logo on Google's screen) needs the public homepage and privacy policy (M9).
  - **Verified during M2 (2026-10-06):** Google gets the SHA-256 hex of the nonce and `signInWithIdToken` the raw nonce (Supabase Google guide); `signInWithIdToken` works with **only the client ID** in Supabase (owner signed in on localhost against the preview project; Google identity recorded, email verified) — no client secret stored in Supabase; a brand-new email-code user receives the "Magic Link" template (local probe), and both templates carry the code anyway; `src/proxy.ts` exports `proxy` (Next 16 docs); nodemailer 10 (maintained, ships its own types); email design uses thick right/bottom borders as the hard shadow because Gmail's mobile apps drop `box-shadow` for Google accounts (caniemail).
  - **Profiles trust only verified identity data:** the user trigger creates an empty profile; name and a Google-hosted avatar are copied from a Google row in `auth.identities` (`identity_data` comes from Google's verified ID token). `raw_user_meta_data` is client-controlled at email sign-up and is never used.
  - **Links the platform sends** (invite emails, copied invite links) use `NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_URL` or this deployment's own `VERCEL_URL` / `VERCEL_BRANCH_URL`, never a request header.
  - **Sessions:** `@supabase/ssr` cookies; `proxy.ts` refreshes the session with `getClaims()`; every protected route re-checks with `getClaims()` (proxy is only an optimistic check); `next=` accepts same-site relative paths only.
  - **Auth emails as code:** subjects and HTML for the auth templates live in the repo (rendered from React Email, same Soft Neobrutalism design as the app within email-client CSS limits) and a versioned script pushes them, plus OTP settings, rate limit, `site_url` and redirect URLs, to both projects via the Management API.
- **Google connection for sending/Sheets:** separate, self-managed OAuth flow on the same client `tapnshow-web`, with **incremental consent**: "Connect Gmail sending" (M4) → `openid email gmail.send` (`gmail.send` is sensitive; `openid email` identifies which account was connected, since `gmail.send` alone cannot); "Connect Google Sheets" (M7) → `drive.file` (non-sensitive).
  - **M4 flow:** `GET /api/integrations/google/connect?workspace=<slug>&next=<path>` (Owner only; state + PKCE + nonce in a short-lived httpOnly cookie, reusing `src/server/auth/google-oauth.ts`) → Google with `access_type=offline`, `prompt=consent select_account`, `include_granted_scopes=true` → `GET /api/integrations/google/callback` (verify state and PKCE, exchange the code with the client secret, read `sub`/`email` from the ID token and the granted `scope`). Google lets users untick individual permissions, so a missing `gmail.send` or a missing refresh token saves nothing and explains how to retry. On success: upsert `google_connections`, set it as the workspace sender, redirect to `next`.
  - **Redirect URIs** to add to `tapnshow-web` (owner, Google Cloud console): `https://tapnshow.vercel.app/api/integrations/google/callback` and `http://localhost:3000/api/integrations/google/callback`.
  - **M7 flow:** the same routes with `purpose=sheets`: scopes `openid email https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.file` with `include_granted_scopes=true` (an account that already sends keeps `gmail.send`); the callback checks the scope its purpose needs (no `drive.file` → nothing saved, plain retry advice); on success it upserts the connection, creates the `sheet_syncs` row without a file and starts a sheet run at once. `drive.file` is non-sensitive (Sheets API scopes page) and accepted by `spreadsheets.create`, `spreadsheets.batchUpdate` and Drive `files.get` (checked 2026-10-10); apps using only non-sensitive scopes need no verification, so M7 adds no verification work. Owner setup once: enable the Google Sheets API and the Google Drive API on project `tapnshow` and add `drive.file` to the consent screen.
  - **Disconnect** stops what the person asked to stop (sending, or the sheet via Turn off); the token is revoked at `https://oauth2.googleapis.com/revoke` (best effort, which removes every scope of the grant) and the row deleted **only once nothing uses the connection** (no workspace sender, no sheet), in one `private` function (M7). Refresh tokens can also stop working when the user revokes access, changes the password, granted time-limited access, or exceeds Google's per-client token count; all surface as `invalid_grant` (§8).
  - **Not used:** `gmail.modify`, `gmail.settings.basic`, `mail.google.com` (restricted scopes, security assessment) — so sent emails cannot be hidden, filtered or deleted; per-meeting threading keeps the Sent folder tidy instead.
- **Google verification:** until verified, organizers see the "unverified app" screen and the app is capped at 100 new users total. Verification requires a public homepage, privacy policy on the same domain, demo video, and Search Console ownership of the domain (S3).
- **Sheets (M7):** the spreadsheet is created by the app in the Drive of the account the Owner picked; the Owner shares it from Google Sheets (§7.17). Sheets API v4 and Drive API v3 through plain `fetch` (no `googleapis` dependency), like Gmail.

## 11. Security & Privacy

- **Authorization:** RLS on every table, with explicit `GRANT`s to `authenticated` only (new tables are not exposed to the Data API by default); API routes use the user's Supabase session so RLS applies; route-level `requireUser()` (`getClaims()`) and `requireRole(workspace, roles)`.
- **`SECURITY DEFINER` exception (M2):** membership/ownership functions (§6) are `SECURITY DEFINER` because their multi-row invariants (one Owner, Owner-only Admin management, invite email match) are fragile under RLS alone. Each checks `auth.uid()` and the caller's role itself, sets `search_path = ''`, has `EXECUTE` revoked from `PUBLIC`/`anon` and granted to `authenticated` only, and is covered by RLS-matrix tests; `supabase db advisors` runs after every migration. **From M3**, `SECURITY DEFINER` bodies live in the non-exposed `private` schema and the Data API sees only thin `public` `SECURITY INVOKER` wrappers with the same signatures (advisor lint 0029 no longer fires; effective security unchanged); new functions are `SECURITY INVOKER` unless an invariant needs more, in which case they follow the same wrapper pattern. The service-role key is used only by the dispatcher, the public token routes (`/r`, `/u`, `/report` APIs), the Gmail connect callback (to save a connection whose Google account id and email come from Google's verified ID token, for the session's user — security review 2026-10-07), `GET /api/health` (its `healthcheck()` function is deliberately not executable by `anon`/`authenticated`), and the pre-auth rate limiter (`check_ip_rate_limit` for sign-in code requests, executable by `service_role` only), each in an isolated server-only module.
- **Public token route:** lookup by hash; rate limited per IP and per token; exposes only that invitee's meeting and response; validates choice against response mode; read-only after start. **M5:** `GET` never writes (link scanners); writes are `PUT`/`POST` with an `Origin` check (only our page calls them; the RFC 8058 unsubscribe `POST` stays the documented exception); the answer page never shows other members' answers; exports escape formula-leading cells.
- **Requests:** `Origin` check on all mutating routes; SameSite=Lax cookies; Zod validation of every body; agenda stored as Markdown and sanitized on render (web and email).
- **Rate limiting:** Postgres-backed (`private.rate_limit_events` + functions, limits in `private.app_limits`) for OTP requests (per IP), workspace creation (5/hour/user), invite emails (100/day platform, 20/day/workspace), token routes, meeting creation (M4: `meetings_per_user_per_hour`), imports (M3: 30 imports, 120 previews and 300 single adds per user per hour). Public token routes (M4) are limited per IP and per token.
- **Logs and error reports:** the logger and Sentry share one scrubbing module (`src/lib/observability/scrub.ts`): values under sensitive field names are redacted at any depth, personal-link tokens and credential-shaped strings (Bearer, JWT, Google OAuth, Supabase secret keys) are scrubbed from every string, errors keep type/message/stack with tokens removed, and scrubbing failures drop the payload (fail closed).
- **Secrets:** Vercel env vars only (Supabase service role, `GOOGLE_TOKEN_ENCRYPTION_KEY`, `INVITE_TOKEN_SECRET`, `DISPATCH_SECRET` (also in Supabase Vault on production, with the dispatch URL), Google client ID/secret, SMTP credentials, VAPID keys); validated at startup by a typed config module; never sent to the client. Every secret is also backed up in the commented blocks of the git-ignored `.env.local`.
- **Emails** (auth codes, invites, later meeting emails) follow the app's Soft Neobrutalism design (outlines, rounded cards, pastel tokens, sticker header) with fallbacks where email clients lack CSS support; never emojis.
- **Headers:** strict CSP, HSTS, `frame-ancestors 'none'`, `Referrer-Policy` that prevents token leakage.
- **Visibility:** every member of a workspace (including Viewers) sees the email addresses of its Owner/Admins/Viewers in Settings > People and of every contact in the roster — stated in the privacy policy (M9). Reasons visible to the workspace's Admins and Viewers only; members never see each other's answers; response form shows "Your answer is visible to <Workspace> organizers".
- **Account deletion:** removes profile, push subscriptions, Google connections, and contact↔account links; erases `reason` and `comment` from that person's responses (and history); statuses remain for workspace statistics, no longer linked to an account. Organizers can delete a contact entirely. The Google Sheet is redrawn from the database, so erased reasons and deleted contacts also leave it at the next redraw (M7); copies people made of it stay out of reach — stated in the privacy policy.
- **Google user data:** privacy policy includes the Limited Use disclosure; only `gmail.send`, and `drive.file` for the one spreadsheet TapNShow creates; besides writing that file, TapNShow reads only its trash status and its own tab metadata; no mail or other Drive files are read. Before connecting, the Owner is told what the sheet holds and that everyone it is shared with sees it (§7.17).
